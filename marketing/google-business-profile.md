# Google Business Profile — what to put in each box

Everything here is copied from the website so the two match. Google compares them.
If a price or an hour changes on the site, change it here and in the profile too.

**Two things never to write: do not say "insured" or "licensed."** Neither is true yet.

---

## Business name

```
Micro Hauling & Junk Removal
```

Exactly that. Do **not** make it "Micro Hauling Provo" or "Provo Junk Removal — Micro
Hauling." Adding towns or keywords to the name violates Google's rules and is the single
most common reason these listings get suspended. The name box is for the name.

## Category

Primary: type `junk` in the category box and pick the closest match Google offers.
Google renames these lists, so choose from what actually appears rather than trusting a
name written here. **Garbage collection service** or **Debris removal service** are the
usual fits.

Add two or three secondary categories from what it suggests, for example furniture
removal, waste management, or hauling. Secondary categories are free reach. The primary
one carries the most weight, so make it the closest match, not the most impressive.

## Address — read this part carefully

The business runs out of a home. When Google asks, choose:

> **I deliver goods and services to my customers**

then answer **No** to showing the address publicly. Google still requires the street
address during signup to verify a real business exists; hiding it keeps it off the public
listing. If you skip this, a home address shows on Google Maps with a pin on the house.

The website deliberately says "Provo, UT" and no street anywhere. Keep it that way.

## Services (with prices)

Google lets you list services and attach a price to each. Filled-in prices are rare in
this trade locally, and they are the whole pitch of the site, so add all three.

| Service | Price | Description |
|---------|-------|-------------|
| One item | $75 | One large item: a couch, a mattress, an appliance, or a desk. Includes labor, carry-out, loading, disposal fees, and a sweep. |
| Half load | $150 | The truck bed full, about a room's worth. Includes labor, carry-out, loading, disposal fees, and a sweep. |
| Full load | $250 | Truck bed and trailer both full, the most one trip carries. Saturdays only. Includes labor, carry-out, loading, disposal fees, and a sweep. |

Add the trailer note somewhere if there is room: needing the trailer for something
smaller than a Full Load is $50 more, Saturdays only.

## Attributes

Tick the ones that are true. Likely: **online estimates**, **onsite services**.
Leave anything about insurance, licensing, or accreditation alone.

## Photos

The listing will not perform without these. Minimum to start:

1. The truck with the trailer hitched, both of you at the tailgate. This is also
   `img/hero-truck.jpg` for the website, so take it once and it does two jobs.
2. A swept "after" shot of a garage corner or room.
3. The two of you, faces visible.
4. The logo, as the profile logo.

Phone camera, daylight, no filters. No house numbers and no customer faces.

## Verification

Service-area businesses are usually asked for a **video** these days, not a postcard.
The recording needs to show, in one unbroken take: the equipment (truck and trailer,
with the plate visible), something identifying the area such as a street sign, and you
holding a tool of the trade or opening the tailgate. Have the truck loaded and clean
before starting. If it asks for a postcard instead, it takes about two weeks.

Verification is a separate thing from the `google*.html` files in the project. Do not
delete those.

## After it goes live

- **Reviews decide the ranking.** Text your first customers the same day, while they
  are still happy, with a direct link to the review form. Five real reviews beats
  anything else on this page.
- **Reply to every review**, including bad ones, briefly and without arguing.
- Never pay for reviews or review your own business. Google detects it and suspends the
  listing.
